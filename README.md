# Cardiovascular Risk Associated with Autoimmune Rheumatic Disease Treatment in Dubai, UAE

Project Type: Clinical Research White Paper  
Research Focus: Cardiovascular Risk · Rheumatology · Medication Safety · Clinical Awareness  
Study Design: Retrospective observational analysis  
Study Population: 981 patients with autoimmune rheumatic disease (ARD)

Overview:

This white paper investigates cardiovascular risk factors associated with medication use among patients with autoimmune rheumatic diseases in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. It examines the relationship between corticosteroid and biologic treatments and the prevalence of hypertension and diabetes mellitus, two important cardiovascular risk factors.

The project aims to improve clinical awareness of potential cardiovascular complications associated with corticosteroid treatment and highlight the importance of monitoring treatment-related risks when managing chronic inflammatory diseases.

Research Question:

How are corticosteroid and biologic treatments associated with the prevalence of hypertension and diabetes mellitus among patients with autoimmune rheumatic diseases in Dubai?

Methods:

- Examined retrospective data from 981 ARD patients.
  
- Compared reported hypertension prevalence among patients receiving biologics and corticosteroids.
  
- Investigated diabetes mellitus prevalence across corticosteroid, biologic, TNF inhibitor, and non-biologic DMARD treatment groups.
  
- Applied two-proportion z-tests using a significance threshold of \(\alpha = 0.05\).
  
- Reviewed relevant literature to contextualize the findings and identify directions for future research.

Key Findings:

- Hypertension: The paper reported hypertension prevalence of 22.23% among patients using biologics and 51.02% among patients using corticosteroids.
  
- Diabetes mellitus: The comparisons did not establish statistically significant differences between the treatment groups examined.
  
- Limited subgroup sizes: Some medication groups contained very few patients, limiting the strength of conclusions that could be drawn.

These findings highlight the importance of investigating cardiovascular risk factors in ARD treatment and communicating potential medication-related risks to healthcare professionals and patients.

Clinical Awareness and Implications:

Corticosteroids are important anti-inflammatory treatments, but their potential metabolic and cardiovascular complications warrant clinical attention. This white paper was developed to increase awareness of these considerations and encourage further investigation into cardiovascular risk monitoring among patients receiving treatment for autoimmune rheumatic diseases.

The findings support continued research into medication-associated risk factors, treatment monitoring, and strategies to improve long-term patient outcomes.

Limitations:

This study uses retrospective observational data and examines associations rather than causal relationships. Differences between treatment groups may reflect patient characteristics, disease severity, treatment duration, or other confounding factors. Some subgroups were small, and the findings should not be generalized beyond the study population without further validation.

The analysis does not establish that biologics are universally safer than corticosteroids or that changing treatment will reduce cardiovascular risk.

Future Directions:

Future research could use larger cohorts, account for treatment duration and disease severity, and investigate longitudinal cardiovascular outcomes. Further work could also examine how clinical education and risk-monitoring practices affect awareness of corticosteroid-associated complications.
